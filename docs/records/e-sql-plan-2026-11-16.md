# E-SQL: the end-to-end plan (2026-11-16)

Stream E-SQL of the total-wall program. The branch is `claude/total-wall-e-sql`. Once the user approves this
plan, it is the scope; anything new goes to §9 unless it blocks the outcome.

## 1. Outcome (the user's words)

> "the leanest fastest most expert SQL for the product, every SQL trick and expert knowledge on every backend to make
> it lean, human readable, fast, and correct"
>
> "subselects only when needed, repetition only when needed, none of the old pure craziness that made us do crazy sql,
> most simple and fast sql everywhere"

The order of databases: DuckDB, Snowflake, Postgres, Databricks, ClickHouse, BigQuery; then MariaDB, MySQL, Trino,
SQLite, H2 and MongoDB.

## 2. Definition of done

All of these are measured, for each database in the user's order. The measures come from the product-SQL baselines:
- channel A: PCT's product queries, about 2,300 statements per database, planned on every database;
- core: core's own product queries;
- the relational corpus: product and judge statements.

Per statement, the measures are characters, subselects and the share of repeated characters. Every number below is
the baseline at the head of the branch, before step 4 (`09eec45`).

### 2.1 Every database, every step

- **Same answers.**
  - Channel A and channel B agree on every lane, with AGREE-FAIL = 0.
  - The corpus rosters do not move.
  - Core's integration lanes are green: DuckDB, H2, SQLite and Postgres locally; the others through a lane round.
- **Shrink-only pins.** The pins only shrink: the baselines, the cast-and-case census and channel B's judge ratchets.
  Any growth has a dated reason in its commit.
- **None slower, by timing.**
  - Each new form is timed against the form it replaces, best of 5-7, on a table of 3M-10M rows.
  - DuckDB, H2, SQLite and Postgres are timed locally; the other databases on their lanes.
  - A form that is not faster or equal is not used on that database.
  - The rule for ties: equal time, smaller text wins; smaller text but slower loses.
- **Readable samples.** Each database gets a set of 5-8 typical queries, written before and after, in its record.

## 6. Out of scope, and where each piece goes

- **Print forms in product SQL.** These are the CASE ladders that rebuild upstream's float text: BigQuery's
  `'Infinity'`/`'NaN'` ladders (8K) and Snowflake's Float shortest-digits text. They belong at D's edge (G5) or are
  deleted by D's print rulings. E-SQL measures them and hands the list to D.
- **The judge's statements and canon** (VerdictSql, testplan, testexec) belong to E-Java. E-SQL's lowering changes
  move the judge's pins, and E-SQL reports each move. Folding a one-row aggregate root into the verdict statement is
  E-Java's (§7 D3).
- **The FULL OUTER emulation's left-side repetition** belongs to the cross-language fix batch. E-SQL takes it as a lean
  item only after that batch lands (§9).
- **Engine-compatible text** (`EngineStyleH2`, DB2, Composite) is stream D's edge and is never changed by E-SQL.
- **Stacked LIMIT and OFFSET** stay as they are (ruled 2026-11-15).

## 7. Decisions for the user, all up front

D1. **The per-database targets in §2.2.**
- Options:
  - (a) approve them as they are;
  - (b) set your own;
  - (c) no numeric targets, only "every named cause worked down or named needed".
- Pros and cons: (a) and (b) give a finish line that can be checked; (c) avoids chasing a number past what is
  sensible.
- Cost: the same either way.
- **Recommendation:** (a), with the record allowed to stop short where a named cause is needed.

D2. **When speed and text size disagree.**
- Options:
  - (a) speed first: a form that is shorter but slower is not used, and on equal speed the shorter form wins;
  - (b) text first, within a tolerance (for example 5% slower).
- Implication: (a) already sent DuckDB back to inline for RowValues, because there the lateral is 38 times slower.
- **Recommendation:** (a). It is the user's "fast" and "lean" in that order, and it is what the code does today.

D3. **A size at the root with no scalar subquery.**
- `SELECT COUNT(*) AS value FROM t` in place of `SELECT (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM t) AS value`.
- The lowering change is written. It grew the judge by 11 statements across two channel-B suites, because the judge
  folds a FROM-less root into its verdict. It needs E-Java to read a one-row aggregate root as one row.
- Options:
  - (a) ask E-Java in its next PR, and E-SQL lands the lowering after it;
  - (b) drop the change.
- Cost: about 2 hours in E-Java and 1 hour here.
- **Recommendation:** (a), in E-v3.

D4. **The plan-format bump in E-v2 (the RowValues tag).**
- Options:
  - (a) keep RowValues in the plan's typed tree, with the version bumped at landing;
  - (b) have the planner write RowValues back inline before the plan's tree is written, so the format does not move.
    Only the statement's text would carry the lateral.
- Pros and cons: (a) keeps the plan's tree true to its statement; (b) avoids colliding with D's numbers.
- **Recommendation:** (a). The orchestrator's numbering rule already handles the collision.

D5. **Cloud timings.**
- Snowflake, Databricks and BigQuery can be timed only on their lanes, with the user's credentials.
- Options:
  - (a) time on the lanes;
  - (b) without timing, those databases keep the inline form for every new binding and change only text-level
    spellings that cannot be slower (IFF for CASE, built-in array functions).
- **Recommendation:** (a) where the lanes are available, else (b). The record says which.

D6. **Who owns the print-form ladders in product SQL (§6).**
- Options:
  - (a) D deletes them or moves them to the edge;
  - (b) E-SQL deletes the ones no product rule names.
- **Recommendation:** (a). D owns print forms by the user's 2026-11-12 ruling. E-SQL hands D the measured list.

## 8. Risks, and how we would know early

- **An answer moves on a lane we cannot run locally** (Snowflake, BigQuery, Databricks). We would know from the E-v2
  lane round. This is why E-v2 asks for every lane, although the external dialects write inline.
- **A lowering rule changes a judge pin or a ratchet** (step 1's root did). We would know when channel B's ratchets and
  the judge baseline fail locally; every step runs them.
- **A form is faster on one size and slower on another.** Each timing runs at two sizes (100K and 10M rows) before a
  form lands.
- **Merge drift with PR1 and D.** E-v2 rebases after PR1, and the re-pins are by measurement, key by key against both
  parents (as lean2's were).
- **The test lane's scan-order emulation reacts to a lowering shape** (it did once, in §3b). We would know from the
  corpus verdicts, run every step.
