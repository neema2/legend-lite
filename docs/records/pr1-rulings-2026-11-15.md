## R3. A mixed list's extreme where elements of different kinds tie: refused by name

Revised 2026-11-16 by the PR1 re-review's B4. The two routes and this record had disagreed; one rule now holds on both
routes and for every family.

- **The expression.** `min`, `max`, `least` or `greatest` over a mixed list that holds two kinds of one family. The
  families are:
  - numbers: `Integer`, `Float`, `Decimal`;
  - dates: `StrictDate`, `DateTime`, `Date`, `DateTime(n)`;
  - enumerations: two enumerations' values.
- **Upstream's answer.** legend-pure orders across kinds with its compare. Elements of different kinds that compare
  equal (1 and 1.0; `%2025-02-10` and `%2025-02-10T00:00:00`) are a tie, and the extreme answers the element of its own
  kind.
- **Lite's answer.** The database orders a tie as it likes, so:
  - Where every element of such a family is a literal, the extreme is **refused only on a real tie**: two literals of
    different kinds that compare equal. `['b', 1, 2.5]->max()` answers, and `['b', 1, 1.0]->max()` is refused.
  - Where an element of such a family is **computed**, its value is not known at planning, so it is answered. A tie it
    makes at run time is a known limit, until the run-time kind carrier (O2 step 3). B4 asked for one rule, "refuse
    only a real literal tie", and on this point the earlier date route's refusal of computed dates is lifted with it.
  - The refusal reads: "the greatest element of a mixed list holding Float and Integer: two of its elements of different
    kinds compare equal, and the extreme of a tie across number kinds is refused until the run-time kind carrier (O2
    step 3) answers it as legend-pure does".
  - A list that isn't written out (a variable's) is answered as a computed one is.
- **Why refused rather than answered.** The same rule as S341's run-time kind. A sort of such a list (D1) still answers;
  only its ends wait.
- **Code.** `Scalars.refuseRealTies`, on both the mixed-elements route and the tagged list's route
  (`TaggedLists.refuseKindTies` is deleted).
- **Pins.**
  - `MixedListConformanceTest.anExtremeAcrossDateKindsIsRefusedByName`;
  - the B4 cases in `aMixedListAnswersAsUpstreamOnEveryDatabase_orIsRefusedByName`;
  - `aMixedListRanksAnExactLiteralAndRefusesAnInexactOne`'s tie cases.

## R4. A computed Integer beyond 2^53 in a mixed list's order: refused by name at run time

Revised 2026-11-16 by the PR1 re-review's A2: it was a known wrong order, and is now a refusal.

- **The expression.** `let n = 9007199254740990 + 3; ['b', $n, 9007199254740991]->sort()`, or `[$n, 0.5]->max()`: a
  mixed list whose integer is COMPUTED, beyond 2^53.
- **Upstream's answer.** legend-pure compares the integers exactly.
- **Lite's answer.** A mixed list's numbers rank as DOUBLE (MixedEncoding's rank).
  - A LITERAL is decided at planning. One the double holds exactly ranks; that includes 2^53 and 2^60, which a magnitude
    bound had refused until B5. One it doesn't hold is refused by name.
  - On a Number list's min/max (B2), two literals the double holds as ONE value (2^53 and 2^53+1; two Decimals that
    round to one double) are refused by name. A Decimal the double merely does not hold (1.23D) still orders right, and
    answers.
  - A COMPUTED Integer is checked by the database. Beyond ±2^53 it raises `VALUE_NOT_HELD`: "an Integer beyond 2^53 in
    a mixed list's order: its DOUBLE rank does not hold it exactly". This is on the sort's route and on a Number list's
    extreme's (B2).
- **Code.** `MixedEncoding.guardedRanks`: the list's ranks guarded ONCE, by one comparison per distinct computed
  Integer expression (the orchestrator's choice (c)), through `Scalars.guarded`. A guard per element measured about 2x
  on a 2,000-element written list, and one `abs()` comparison per element about +50%. The single guard measured within
  noise on DuckDB: 115-130 ms against 152-185 ms unguarded, best of 9.
- **The `range()`/`concatenate()` route (checked 2026-11-16).** It does not rank through this DOUBLE rank, so the guard
  isn't on it. It has wrong answers of its own, the variant carrier's, which are O2 step 3's to fix (not checked
  against main):
  - `range(9007199254740990, 9007199254740996)->reverse()->concatenate(['a'])->sort()` answers `'a'` first, where Pure
    orders numbers before strings.
  - `...->concatenate([2.5])` carries every element as a DOUBLE. Integers lose their kind and precision
    (9007199254740993 becomes 9007199254740992.0), and the sort and max answer those values.
- **Pins.**
  - `MixedListConformanceTest.aComputedIntegerBeyond2To53InAMixedRankRaisesAtRun`: it raises, and a value within 2^53
    answers.
  - `aMixedListRanksAnExactLiteralAndRefusesAnInexactOne`.

## R6. The always-evaluated set widened through strict calls

- **The ruling.** The orchestrator ruled under the user's delegation of 2026-11-15. A raising repeat may be bound
  where one copy is always evaluated. The set of always-evaluated nodes now descends through every strict call. It
  never descends into a rendering that can skip an argument on any of the 13 dialects:
  - AND, OR, COALESCE;
  - every function a dialect renders with a branch: CASE, ClickHouse's `if`/`multiIf`, IIF, IF, DECODE
    (`SqlFn.MAY_SKIP_AN_ARGUMENT`, read from the dialects' render arms);
  - a CASE's later conditions and branches;
  - lambda bodies, subqueries, aggregates, windows, folds, and the tagged, compacted, checked and membership carriers.
- **How.** `LetBinding.alwaysEvaluated` is one exhaustive switch over `SqlExpr`'s variants, with no default arm.
- **Test.** `LetBindingTest.theAlwaysEvaluatedSetCrossesStrictCallsAndNeverASkippingOne`.
- **Measured.** The date family's MAKE_TIMESTAMP, raising since item 9, is bound again. Its size falls BELOW the
  pre-item-9 pin: core DuckDB 4548 to 1808 characters. Channel A's case `|2015->date(4, 16, 14, 51, 59.999)`:
  SQLite 3545 to 1052, Trino 2384 to 712. The core baseline re-pinned with no family grown.
- **Known limit.** The render-arm reading is a census of each arm's own body. A branch written in a dialect's helper
  method would be missed. A render arm that starts branching must join `MAY_SKIP_AN_ARGUMENT`.
