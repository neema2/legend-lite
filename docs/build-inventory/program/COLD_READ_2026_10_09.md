# A fresh read of the whole bump program, end to end (2026-10-09) — for the user's decision

Written at the user's request after D24 (1) landed ("step back and relook at whole bump program with fresh eye, make
sure the whole plan makes sense end to end, ordered correctly, and we know what to do to make it really well done like a
real programming language"). Read: the plan (`docs/REBUILD_PROGRAM_2026_10_06.md`), the design
(`docs/BUILD_REBUILD_DESIGN_2026_10_05.md`), `docs/GENERATORS.md`, the four execution briefs, the earlier cold read
(`COLD_READ_2026_10_07.md`), the debts research (`DEBTS_RESOLVE_AND_TYPE_ONCE.md`), the boundary program
(`docs/UPSTREAM_BOUNDARY_PROGRAM.md`), the prelude design, the manifest-world experiments, the Phase 3 branch's code and
ledger, and today's main (`Compiler.boot`, `PlatformRegistrations`, `Bump.java`, the generators, the pins). Nothing
below changes a plan or a brief; each finding names what it would change and leaves the ruling to the user.

## 1. The verdict, in one paragraph

The program's shape is the right one for a language toolchain: a pinned specification (the two upstream archives),
declaration tables generated from it, a hand-written implementation table keyed by a stable identity (the function
id), a compiler that consults ids and never names, a standard library that is upstream's own source compiled by the
same compiler, and a bump that is "move one number, regenerate, seal, judge". That is how a mainstream compiler treats
its standard library and its intrinsics. The order is mostly right too (2b informed 3; 3 before 6 and 4; 6 before 4;
5 after 4; 7 last). Three things are not yet right, and each is cheap to fix now and expensive later: (1) the two
front-end invariants a real compiler builds first, "every name resolved once" and "every argument typed once", are
scheduled after the bump (L7, L9) although Phases 4 and 5 are exactly the phases that change where names come from;
(2) the north star's "generated only by the bump" cannot hold for the default world, which is generated through our
own parser, and the plan's own design already says so (group B) while the plan and the seal do not; (3) Phase 3 is
231 commits behind main and no longer "the five commits as they are": it must be re-judged whole before it lands.
Everything else is detail, and the detail is in good order: the briefs are honest about what was measured and what
was inferred, and the open decisions are listed, not hidden.

## 3. Findings, by weight

### F1. The front end's two invariants come last; "resolve once" should come before Phases 4 and 5

**What the plan says.** PARK-5 ("a call to a platform function is never resolved once": every check re-derives a bare
call's meaning from its spelling under 32 packages, 19% of typing on main and 29% after Phase 3) is fixed at L7, after
Phase 5, with the reason "Phases 4 and 5 touch the same ~200 call sites and change where declarations come from; the
fix is done once, on the final shape". PARK-6 ("arguments typed more than once", seven places) and the ranking
departures (PARK-7 to 10, 14) are L9, after the bump.

**Why the reason is backwards for PARK-5.** The final shape of a call is "it carries its resolved declaration id"
(PARK-5's own acceptance; the boundary program's standing ruling "a function's identity is its FunctionId, compared
whole"; the compiler plan's W2.3a). Once calls carry ids, Phases 4 and 5 change only HOW the declaration table is
populated (from the generated world instead of `Pure.all()`), and no call site at all. Done the other way round,
Phases 4 and 5 rewrite the ~200 call-building sites against the bare-name mechanism, and L7 rewrites them again. The
same holds for the judge: every lane the bump's phases must keep identical is cheaper to hold when the resolver, not
five spelling checks, says what a call means. Phase 3 itself made the cost visible (+18% typing) precisely because it
did the right thing (forms read by resolved names) on top of the wrong mechanism.

**What it would change.** L7 (the full fix, option (c) of `DEBTS_RESOLVE_AND_TYPE_ONCE.md`) moves between L3 (Phase 3b)
and L4 (Phase 6): the resolver resolves every bare call to platform functions once and records the ids on the call;
the calls the typer and the normalizer build after the resolver are built resolved; `ResolvedNames` reads the record;
`BareNames.catalog` goes. Size: the resolver, about 200 call-building sites, 10 readers of `candidateFqns()`; its own
design and audit, as the ledger says. It is the compiler plan's W2.3a push 2c in a smaller coat, so it should be
claimed by this program in IN_FLIGHT and in the compiler plan (as Phase 3 claimed W2.1).

**PARK-6 and the ranking departures (L9).** These decide WHICH overload a user's call binds to; they are semantics,
not cleanup, and legend-pure is the oracle. Keeping them after the bump is defensible only because the reference lane
holds them to a measured residue (OVERLOAD 745 to 58 on Phase 3's branch; 3b item 3 reviews the 58). Two conditions
make it safe: the reference lane runs before every landing from L2 on (the briefs already say so), and "done means" for
the program includes L9, not only the bump. Recommendation: keep L9 where it is, write it into §0.6.

### F2. "Generated only by the bump" cannot hold for the default world; the design already says so, the plan and the seal do not

**The contradiction.** The north star (plan §1): generated files are "made from upstream and one setting, only by the
bump, committed and sealed". The design (§4.2) splits generators into group A (upstream only, the bump) and group B
("upstream plus our parser or our committed registries: a parser change is a real trigger here"), and the Phase 4
brief's D4-5 says the default-world generator must resolve names, which needs a parser, and that if it is ours "a
parser change can change the world without a bump and the seal will not see it". The plan's §0.2 names this as "one
open point" and leaves it.

**What a toolchain does.** The library snapshot is derived from the specification by the toolchain's own front end;
that is normal (a compiler's precompiled standard library is built by that compiler). What matters is that the
derivation is a declared build step whose inputs include the front end, so a front-end change re-derives it and a
stale snapshot is caught by the build, not by a hash of itself.

**What it would change.** Three sentences: (a) group A records (DynaFn, handlers, imports, fixtures, manifests, vocab)
are the bump's and sealed, as planned; (b) the default world is a group B record: generated from the pinned archives,
the module choice and OUR parser and resolver, regenerated by the bump AND whenever the parser or resolver changes,
held by a diff test in `//:generated` whose inputs name those libraries (so a parser change that moves the world fails
loudly and a person regenerates, no bump needed); (c) the seal records the module choice (D7-2) and, for the world, the
version of the generator's inputs it was made with (the pins and the generator library's own hash), so a seal that
passes says "made from these pins by this generator". D4-5 is then decided: our parser, and the diff test is what the
seal cannot be. This also settles design D3 (parsing upstream with upstream's parser): not needed.

### F3. Phase 4's D4-1 — follow every body; never let the generator read what we implement

The measured closure decided which upstream bodies to follow by reading `Pure.java`, `CoreFn` and `WalledBodies`
(`closure.py:61-77`), which the north star forbids. The upstream-only rule is "follow every body" (+80 names, +74 KB at
the measured size; never yet run through the corpus, PCT and the user side). Recommendation: rule D4-1 that way now.
Pruning by what the platform implements is the implementation table's job at boot (a row says "ours", no row means
upstream's body runs; a NO_ROW refusal is the loud case), never the generator's. The extra 80 names are the price of
a world that is a function of upstream alone, and the price is small. The first Phase 4 homework (U4-1, the real world
measured) should be run on this rule.

### F4. Phase 3 is no longer "the five commits as they are"

`build/phase3` is 231 commits behind main. A merge preview (`git merge-tree origin/main origin/build/phase3`) shows
content conflicts in `native-claims.tsv`, `ParkedWorkLedgerTest.java`, the ledger, GATES and the compiler plan, and
auto-merges in `Typer`, `InferenceKernel`, `UserCallInliner`, `SourceSubst`, `TdsDesugars`, `NativeFn`, `Pure`,
`SystemMetamodel`, `PlatformTypes`, `CoreFn`, `Implementation`. Since the branch was judged, main gained the erased
TDS row (the typer), W1.5 (every collection's iteration order), W0.8 (the lexer), D24 (the inliner rewritten over
`TypedSubst`), the protocol program's three legs (the parser and the reader), and the dialect writer. An auto-merge of
`Typer` and `InferenceKernel` is not a judgement. L2 therefore is: rebase, resolve the five conflicts, then the whole
judgement again — the local gate, the six corpus passes against a fresh baseline, PCT, the reference lane re-blessed
with reasons, and the render census before and after (the judge D24 established; `census-judges-compiler-changes`).
Budget it as a landing of its own size, not "one CI run".

### F5. Two plans own the same front end; give each item one owner

The compiler plan (`docs/EXECUTION_PLAN_2026_09_26.md`, resumed by D25 on 2026-10-07) and the bump program overlap
exactly where the bump needs the compiler: W2.1 (ids, the catalog; taken over by Phase 3, recorded), W2.3a (the
resolved model; PARK-5's fix is its push 2c), W3.4 (G½ substitutes, never re-types; PARK-6's family), W4.4a (load by
manifest; Phase 6), D9 (the manifest world; answered by the experiments). Since 2026-10-07 the compiler plan has landed
four items on main (W1.0b, W0.8, a W1.5 slice, D24 (1)), all useful, none of which the bump needed, while Phase 3
waited. Recommendation: one rule, written in both plans and IN_FLIGHT: the bump's landings L2 to L8 own the front end
(resolution, overloads, the world, the tables); the compiler plan continues only with items that do not touch those
(W0.6's pushes, the wrong-rows tool, the lexer, the lowering's cleanups), and its D24 second slice (the syntax-level
policies) waits for L7, which changes the same resolver.

### F6. The implementation table: right shape; the 109 unrowed versions are the bump's real work, size them

Five row kinds (Form, Intrinsic, Body, Unimplemented, Refused with a reason) plus the planned "platform's own Pure"
(3b-O1 (b)) is the right vocabulary: builtin, library body, our body, refused-with-reason, and language forms. The
rule "a version of a function the platform declares with no row of its own is refused, never run from upstream's body"
is the mechanism that makes a bump loud, and it is good. Its consequence is 109 upstream versions without a row
(`implementation.unrowed`), each of which gets a row or a recorded refusal before Phase 4 lands (D4-12). That is 109
decisions a person makes, with the reference lane as the oracle; it is the bump program's largest hand-written
deliverable and no brief sizes it. Recommendation: size it (a day per 30 rows is the Phase 3 rate: 27 rows came with
the branch), and let the rows live in one file a reviewer reads top to bottom.

### F7. Boot: plan for the pre-built boot layer, decide it on numbers

The default world grows from 641 to 929 elements (floor: the measured world still dropped functions Phase 3 keeps):
+74 ms JVM, +590 ms to the browser's first answer, where today's parse of `prelude.pure` alone is 193 ms and the boot
layer's resolve, normalize and index 835 ms (56% of the browser's start). Decision 2 (profile first) is right. But the
destination is known from every real toolchain: a precompiled library (the compiler plan's W2.1 "a serialized resource,
not 854 parses at class load"; the plan's Phase 4b). It is a build output of our compiler over the default world, never
a bump record, never committed. Recommendation: keep decision 2's order (profile, remove redundant work), and write 4b
into the plan as the expected outcome whose go is the measured number, so the Phase 4 design leaves room for it (one
boot artifact, content-addressed, the same bytes in the JVM and the WASM planner).

### F8. "Done means" is missing three lines

§0.6 should also say: (a) the identity guard's dispatch counts are 0 (no string identity for a resolved function; the
untangle's step 5, which the table makes possible and L7 starts); (b) L9's acceptance (overloads and types as
legend-pure's, the departures closed or registered); (c) the run-against-run comparison exists as a Bazel tool (the
Phase 8 brief says it is "not designed yet"; the render census built for the plan-execution split and used by D24 is
its working prototype: two census directories, one `compare.py`, every statement every lane renders; make it a
`bazel run` tool and a lane artifact in L12).

### F10. The bar: what "well done like a real programming language" asks for, and where each piece is

- A specification and a reference: upstream's archives and legend-pure's and legend-engine's answers (the corpus,
  PCT, the reference lane, parser parity). In place. The deliberate differences live in `docs/SEMANTICS_REGISTER.md`;
  make it normative (every departure, PARK-7 to 10 and 14 included, has a row or is closed).
- One resolution, one typing, overloads by the reference's rule: F1 (L7, L9).
- Intrinsics by stable identity, the library as source: Phases 3, 5 (the table), 4 (the world). Right.
- A precompiled library and a content-addressed boot: F7.
- Deterministic, sealed derived artifacts, the same bytes on three platforms: `//:generated` today, the seal
  tomorrow (F2).
- Diagnostics with positions and a verifier between passes: the compiler plan's W1.2 and W1.3; not this program's,
  and they should stay after it (F5).
- Tests per family, each on what it exercises; no host programs; no Node: Phase 8 (L10 to L12). Right.
- No string identity for a resolved symbol: the untangle's step 5; make it an exit test (F8).
