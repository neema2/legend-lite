## 4. The two caps

**GitHub's cache: 10 GB per repository.** `gh api repos/<owner>/<repo>/actions/cache/usage` on 2026-10-07:
9,947,564,258 bytes active in 3 entries, all macOS (`build` 6.78 GB, `6` 1.60 GB, `9` 1.57 GB). The `PHASE_8.md`
brief's 5 entries / 17.5 GB of the same morning had been evicted down to the cap. The key is per platform, image, pins
and lane (`gates-run.yml`), so one lane's tarball of outputs crowds out every other; Linux and Windows have no entry at
all and start cold on every run. A cache of the downloads alone (`~/.cache/bazel-repo`) fits: the whole tarball of a
small lane, downloads and outputs together, is 1.6 GB.

**GitHub runs at most 5 macOS jobs at once.** `concurrency.py` over the run's jobs: Linux peaked at 16 concurrent
jobs, Windows at 13, macOS at exactly 5; macOS's last job started 27 minutes after its first, and the platform's span
was 37 minutes with no lane longer than 25. On macOS the wall clock is the queue: roughly the sum of the lane minutes
divided by 5; every extra macOS lane costs its ~2-minute floor divided by 5.

## 5. The native image is built three times per platform

`//warehouse:server_native` is a dependency of the `build` lane (`//...`), the `native` lane (`tests_native`) and the
`browser` lane (`live_snap_test`, `//query:verify`). Per-lane caches cannot share it under the cap; only a remote
cache would (`PHASE_8.md` OD-5).

## 6. What was concluded (the plan's §4, L2; `PHASE_8.md` §8)

The product's own compile is a few minutes (`//:java` 12.4 s clean on a laptop, `//:web` 4.8 s; the native image and
the WebAssembly build a few minutes each); the build lane was slow because it built everything, including the six
corpus passes (which lanes 4 and 5 then compute again) and the 2 GB `//pct:ratchets`. The browser lane was slow
because its harnesses are programs run one at a time by a shell loop. The per-lane floor is cold fetching. Hence L2:
the build lane builds the product; every lane is a `//gates` suite; the harnesses are tests; the downloads are cached
per platform; the heavy and hand targets are `manual`.

## 8. The second run (37656436656, L1b, 2026-10-07 17:06 to 17:38 UTC): cold again, and why

Green, all 51 jobs; wall clock 32 minutes; `product` 20 / 8 / 19, `datacube` 15 / 11 / 23, `checks` 9 / 9 / 16. Not
warm: every platform's product job logged `Cache not found` or a wrong restore.
- The key held the runner image's version (`bazel-repo-<platform>-<image>-<pins>`), and GitHub's hosted runners rotate
  between image versions run to run: within an hour, Linux came up as `20261004.327.1` and `20260927.320.1`, Windows as
  `20260927.320.1` and `20261004.326.1`. A key with the image in it almost never matches twice.
- The restore's prefix fallback `bazel-repo-linux-` matched `bazel-repo-linux-arm-…`: Linux restored the arm64
  downloads (2 minutes), useless to x86_64, then fetched everything cold.
- `gh cache list` after the run: Linux **19.18 GB**, Windows 5.08 GB, macOS 5.02 GB — 29 GB against the 10 GB cap, so
  entries are evicted between runs. The product job saved after `bazel build --nobuild //...`, which fetches every
  jar of every Maven pool (the engine's full stack among them) and every archive the repository can reach: a cache of
  the *product's* downloads is what every lane needs; the test pools are each lane's own.

The fix (L1b's follow-up, `gates-run.yml`): the key `bazel-repo-<platform>=<pins>` (no image; `=` so one platform's
prefix cannot match another's), restore-keys `bazel-repo-<platform>=`, and the save step right after the product
build, before the analysis of everything. Its run measures each platform's cache; if the product's downloads alone do
not fit the cap, GitHub's cache is the wrong tool and OD-5 is decided as a remote cache.
