Reading
-------
The flag's gain is the core lane's, as PR #44 measured: its many short-lived JVMs (the 40 targets, all under 300 s)
spend more CPU in C2 than in their tests. Its cost is the few long JVMs, which C2 repays: with C1 alone
parser_parity runs 743 s on Linux against its 900 s limit (412 s before; 706 s on macOS from 529; 597 s on Windows
from 476), stress_suites_h2 723 s on Linux (205) and 835 s on Windows (188), round_trip_proof 198 s on macOS (129);
the stress job 7.9 -> 17.4 min on Linux and 4.8 -> 15.8 on Windows, parser_equivalence 12.5 -> 18.0 and
13.8 -> 17.8; the gate's longest job grows from 13 to 17-18 minutes. The PCT lanes are mixed (pct_duckdb_relation
69 -> 95 s, the channel B targets faster). PR #46's "no target slower by more than 20%" holds for the core lane it
measured, not for the lanes its own run added.

Verdict: not as a global test:ci flag. The JIT choice is the target's, in tools/junit/defs.bzl: junit_test puts
-XX:TieredStopAtLevel=1 in every test JVM's flags by default, and a target measured slower names jit = "c2" with
its numbers (stress_suites, stress_suites_h2, parser_parity, round_trip_proof; a PCT target by its own measurement).
The same on a desk and in CI: one JVM per target everywhere, and no test:-only option that changes the launchers
between a job's build and test commands (the warehouse lane builds its native images, then tests).
