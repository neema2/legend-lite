## 2. What they say

- **F1, fixed: a basket of 500 overflowed the typer's stack.** The translator chained the members' long relation
  left-deep, 500 `concatenate`s; `TsLines.balanced` puts N relations together as a balanced tree, log2 N deep (and the
  spread's instruments and periods alike). 238b6921b.
- **F2, fixed (414d6cc8d): as-of was quadratic per line on every database without an ASOF JOIN** (Postgres, H2,
  SQLite, MariaDB, BigQuery). Postgres ran lite's correlated `MAX` once per left row (the plan: `SubPlan`,
  `loops=5040`), not per pair -- but each probe scanned the line's materialized CTE, which no index reaches: 5,040 ×
  5,040 rows for one line, 8.2 s, with or without the table's index. `LATERAL` is no better (the same probe of the
  same CTE); the hand forms are fast only because they probe the indexed base table. The fix, plain SQL on every such
  database (`CarrierStrategies.asOfByWindow`): the two sides' keys and dates as one stream ordered by keys and date,
  each left date's match a running `MAX` over a window, then an equality join on it -- one sort, O(n log n). One line
  on Postgres: 6,968 ms -> 472 ms without the index, 6,573 ms -> 52 ms with it, as fast as the experts' hand forms.
  Every line with as-of is still past 90 s there, as every hand form is: Postgres's cost at this size (F4).
  `AsOfJoinCheckerTest` pins the form on H2 and SQLite; PCT on Postgres, H2 and SQLite and the H2 corpus pass.
- **F6, fixed (49f963b44): a line's values met the answer's dates by a nested loop on Postgres.** The daily one-line
  query explained (its time the line's, not the table's: 2.9 s at 1M rows as at 10M): two `Nested Loop Left Join`s,
  each filtering 25,396,560 rows (5,040 × 5,040) on `NOT (a IS DISTINCT FROM b)`. The library's `onto`, `within` and
  `meet` compared a line's periods and keys read as optional values, which lower null-safe (as legend-engine's
  `nullSafeEqualsOperation` does), and Postgres can neither hash nor merge on a null-safe equality. A line's keys and
  periods are never empty (its rows are filtered to present keys and dates), so they compare `->toOne()`: a plain `=`,
  merged. At 10M rows: one line 3,607 ms -> 573 ms (with the index 2,922 -> 146), 20 items 11,523 -> 1,412, a basket
  of 100 past 90 s -> 83,585 ms; the rest as before.
- **F3, open: on Postgres the 100 lines' query (428 KB of SQL) grew one backend to 11 GB**, and the session's memory
  limit killed it (`dmesg`: oom-kill, postgres, anon-rss 11,011,560 kB). Not repeated on a desk; the Postgres target
  runs no wide table and a basket of 100 (`ts.scale.wideLines`, `ts.scale.basket`). Tried again after F6 at 1M rows
  (2f5294808): killed the same way -- the statement's, not the data's. To find out: the planner or the executor, and
  what in the spread's SQL.
- **F4: after F2 and F6, Postgres is about 7 to 10 times DuckDB's time on one line** (573 ms; 146 ms with the index;
  DuckDB 81 ms) **and 20 to 30 on every line of the table** (every line 56 s, DuckDB 2.9 s; a basket of 100, 84 s, DuckDB's
  of 500 9.8 s), a row store reading 10M rows; as-of on every line is past 90 s (so is every hand form). The next to
  read: the every-line plans, and a basket's.
- **F5: the SQL grows about 4.3 KB a line** (100 lines, 428 KB; a basket of 500, 334 KB): the lean-SQL work.
- **Not measured yet:** the tile's cold open with 100,000 points (B2's other half), with item 8 (the class page).
